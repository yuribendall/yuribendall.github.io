How Materials, Aerodynamics & Avionics Intersected

Stage 01 · Materials

Anisotropic Balsa & Carbon Spar Architecture

When you build an aircraft structure that weighs only a few hundred grams, materials science is everywhere. Two sheets of balsa wood that look identical at a glance can vary wildly in density and stiffness depending on how the timber was cut relative to the growth rings. Pairing a C-grain balsa shear web with pultruded carbon-fiber spar caps creates a miniature I-beam with an exceptional stiffness-to-weight ratio.

Stage 02 · Fabrication

Adhesive Mass Budgeting & Torsional Stiffness

In micro-class aero competitions, empty weight scales non-linearly with stall speed and takeoff roll. Wicking too much cyanoacrylate or epoxy into a joint adds dead mass without increasing bond strength, because the surrounding wood fibers will tear long before the glue joint fails. Every bond line was metered to keep structural mass on budget.

Stage 03 · Mechatronics

Propulsion Sizing & Zero-Ballast CG

On the mechatronics side, we co-located the battery, receiver, and micro-servos with the main structural bulkhead so the aircraft balanced at its aerodynamic center of gravity naturally—eliminating the need for any non-structural nose weight.
