About Me

A visual, systems-minded engineer who likes connecting the dots.

In an industry where people often specialize into a single narrow lane, my strength is understanding how the broader physical system fits together—from the microstructure of a material to the electronics on the board and the logistics required to ship it.

01 · Perspective

Why the Whole System Matters

Why I chose Materials Engineering to anchor my interest in hardware and mechatronics.

What drives me isn't just solving isolated equations in a vacuum—it's the visual and structural elegance that emerges when complex physics interact in a complete, tangible system. Knowing only the hyper-specialized math of one sub-component doesn't make hardware succeed if you don't understand what it connects to.

Joining the Materials Engineering faculty at UBC gave me the missing link between mechanical structures and electronics. Materials science gives you the invisible rules of why a solder joint fatigues, why a copper trace undercuts during chemical etching, or how mineral phases fracture; mechatronics and hardware prototyping make those rules tangible.

02 · Track Record

What I’ve Built & Learned

Hands-on hardware, team leadership, lab experimentation, and hard-won business lessons.

I've always learned best by jumping into ambitious projects. In high school, I served as the technical lead for a capstone project building an automated, self-sustaining terrarium to support a biological research team studying bacterial-assisted plant survival. I handled all the 3D printing, wiring, soldering, and electronics while recruiting and managing two other students on software and custom enclosure carpentry.

In my first year at UBC, I joined UBC Aero Design on the Power & Controls subteam. I quickly took ownership of the micro-class aircraft's electrical harness, custom battery assemblies, wind-tunnel propulsion testing, and flight-line operations at the SAE competition, before stepping up as Power & Controls Lead over the summer to manage PCB design, propeller simulation, and subteam budgeting.

In the lab, I've characterized multi-phase mineral specimens using SEM, XRD, and optical microscopy, and even worked in Dr. David Dixon's hydrometallurgy lab to chemically etch my own custom PCB from bare copper laminate (which taught me both how chemical etching works up close and that pure hydrometallurgy isn't my forever home).

Outside the university, I started my own hardware company importing products from China to sell in Canada because I wanted to understand supply chains and business operations firsthand. When early beta testing uncovered product defects and regulatory certification issues, I made the tough call to absorb the loss, halt sales, and responsibly recycle the inventory rather than risk a customer's safety or my reputation. It was an expensive crash course, but it taught me more about real-world DFM, compliance, and quality control than any textbook could.

03 · Direction

Where I Want to Go Next

Contributing to hardware, R&D, and materials-driven engineering teams.

I'm looking for engineering roles and internships where I can combine hands-on hardware prototyping, materials characterization, and systems-level problem solving.

Because I've sat on both sides of the table—soldering harnesses and running SEM scans in the lab, as well as dealing with contract manufacturers, budgets, and compliance roadblocks—I love working on multidisciplinary teams where engineering decisions have to work both on the benchtop and in the real world.
